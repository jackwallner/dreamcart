How it's built and why it matters.

The Pitch

Shopping feels good long before the package arrives. Browsing, choosing, filling a cart, checking out, unboxing: that's the loop, and the credit card bill is just the part bolted onto the end.

DreamCart keeps the loop and drops the bill. Coins arrive free every day, the catalog is original virtual fashion, and everything you check out with joins a closet you can style. No merchandise exists, and none is shipped.

Coins

DreamCart is free to play. Coins arrive from the daily drop, the free daily wheel, and streaks.

Optional coin packs. Consumable packs from $1.99 to $49.99 are available through Apple for players who want to shop faster. They are for in-app use only, have no cash value, and are never required to keep playing.

No paid tier. There is no subscription and no locked content: every world, every drop, and the whole closet are open to free players.

What Mattered

Virtual, stated everywhere. Checkout, delivery, and the closet all say plainly that nothing physical is sold. A shopping game that's coy about that is a trap.

Free coins first. The daily drop and wheel are generous enough that coin packs feel like impatience, not a wall.

An original catalog. Brands, pieces, and artwork are generated for the app, so nothing leans on a real retailer's name or imagery.

Progress that survives. Closet, coins, and streaks live server-side, so a new phone doesn't wipe a year of hauls.

DreamCart is a game. All items, coins, checkout, and delivery are imaginary and have no cash value. No physical products are sold or shipped, and optional coin packs are for in-app use only.
